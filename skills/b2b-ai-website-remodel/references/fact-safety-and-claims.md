# Fact Safety And Claims

B2B manufacturing websites can lose trust if the agent invents capability, certification, or export claims. Treat every important business statement as evidence-bound.

## Fact Labels

Use:

- `verified`: appears in source materials or the user explicitly confirms it.
- `inferred`: likely based on context but not proven.
- `missing`: should exist for trust or AI readability but is unavailable.

Publish only verified facts. Inferred facts can appear in a question list or draft marked for approval.

## High-Risk Claims

Require explicit evidence:

- ISO, CE, RoHS, REACH, FDA, UL, SGS, TUV, IATF, GMP, food grade, medical grade, flame retardant, waterproof, eco-friendly, recyclable, biodegradable.
- Factory area, employee count, production lines, monthly capacity, machinery count, years of experience.
- Export countries, named clients, brand partners, case studies.
- MOQ, lead time, warranty, price, inventory, shipping terms.
- Test results, tolerances, purity, grade, load capacity, safety, performance.

## Safe Wording

When evidence is incomplete:

- Use "available on request" only if the company can provide it.
- Use "supports custom requirements" only if customization is verified.
- Use "designed for" only if product use cases are shown or confirmed.
- Prefer "ask our team about..." instead of making unsupported claims.

## Owner Questions

Ask direct questions:

- Are you the manufacturer, trading company, or both?
- Which products should the homepage prioritize?
- Which certifications can be published?
- What MOQ and lead time can be shown publicly?
- Can buyers send drawings or samples?
- Which countries or industries can be mentioned?
- Which claims must stay exact for legal or compliance reasons?
